What you are looking at

A creature whose skin is the whole Earth, bent into a ring. Its outer rim is the equator, its back is the northern hemisphere, its belly the southern, and both poles meet in its throat. The Sun is where the Sun really is, so the planet turns beneath it — one slow lap a day. Day chases night around the body and never catches it.

Nothing here is simulated. Everything that moves, moves because something real just happened. Your browser is listening directly to six public sources; when one falls silent, that part of the creature goes still.

Drag to circle it. Pinch or scroll to come close. Tap anything to ask what it is.

  • Fur — the livingEvery hair is a plant, bird, insect or fungus that somebody just noticed and shared, growing where it was seen. Colour is its kind. They arrive about a minute behind the world. Watch long enough and the continents grow in, out of attention alone.iNaturalist community · api.inaturalist.org
  • Sway and drifting threads — the airThe fur leans and sways with the real wind at that spot, and pale threads drift where the air is going. Direction and relative strength are real; the pace is exaggerated so you can see it.Open-Meteo · CC BY 4.0
  • Embers and shivers — the groundEvery earthquake of the past day glows where it struck: size is magnitude, colour is depth (amber shallow, violet deep). A new one sends a real ripple through the body, and the last half hour still trembles.U.S. Geological Survey · public domain
  • Breath — the writtenEach spore leaving the throat is a page being born on Wikipedia or a sister project, this second. Each language breathes out in its own direction; longer pages drift further. Gold was written by a person, blue diamonds by bots.Wikimedia EventStreams
  • The white traveller — people in orbitThe International Space Station, with its crew aboard, circling the body every ninety-two minutes.wheretheiss.at
  • Dust and the glowing throat — the SunThe dust crosses the space at the measured speed of the solar wind, true to the scale of the body. The green fire in the throat is the aurora, where NOAA's model says it is burning right now.NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center · public domain

The faint band around the body is roughly your own meridian, guessed from your clock's timezone — nothing about you is collected or sent anywhere. The layers share a body, not a cause: any pattern you see between them is yours to wonder about.
