What you are looking at

The ring is the whole Earth, seen from above the north pole and opened out: the inner edge is the Arctic, the outer edge is Antarctica. The Sun is always at the top, so the planet turns beneath it — one slow lap a day. Day chases night around the ring and never catches it.

Nothing on it is simulated. Your browser is listening directly to six public sources. When one falls silent, that part of the creature simply goes dark.

  • Embers — the groundEvery earthquake of the past day, where it struck. Size is magnitude; colour is depth (amber shallow, violet deep). New ones arrive with a shockwave.U.S. Geological Survey · public domain
  • Drifting threads — the airWind near the surface, right now, on a coarse global grid. Direction and relative strength are real; the speed is exaggerated so you can see it.Open-Meteo · CC BY 4.0
  • Coloured motes — the livingA plant, bird, insect or fungus that somebody just noticed and shared. They arrive about a minute behind the world. Watch long enough and the continents draw themselves, out of attention alone.iNaturalist community · api.inaturalist.org
  • The seed-head at the centre — the writtenEach stalk is a page being born on Wikipedia or a sister project, this second. Each language grows in its own direction; longer pages reach further. Gold seeds were written by people, blue diamonds by bots.Wikimedia EventStreams
  • The white traveller — people in orbitThe International Space Station, with its crew aboard, lapping the ring every ninety-two minutes.wheretheiss.at
  • Falling dust & glowing edges — the SunThe specks drift at the measured speed of the solar wind, true to the scale of the planet. The green fire on the ring's edges is the aurora, where NOAA's model says it is burning right now.NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center · public domain

Touch the ring to ask what is there. The small notch on the outer edge is roughly your own meridian, guessed from your clock's timezone — nothing about you is collected or sent anywhere. The layers share a canvas, not a cause: any pattern you see between them is yours to wonder about.
